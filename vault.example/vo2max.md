# VO2max

The maximum rate at which the body can take up and use oxygen during intense
exercise, expressed in ml/kg/min. One of the classic ceilings on endurance
performance, alongside lactate threshold and running economy.

## Key points

- Higher VO2max raises the aerobic ceiling, but race performance also depends
  heavily on the **fraction** of VO2max you can sustain (threshold) and on
  economy — two runners with equal VO2max can race very differently.
- It responds to training, especially work near VO2max intensity (intervals at
  ~95–100% of VO2max pace, e.g. 3–5 min repeats), but improvements plateau as
  training age increases.
- Estimates from wrist devices are approximations from HR/pace relationships;
  treat them as trend lines, not lab values.

## Training implications

- VO2max sessions are potent but costly — space them (every ~10–14 days in a
  block) and protect easy days so [[training-load-atl-ctl-tsb]] stays sustainable.
- Base aerobic volume raises the ceiling that intervals then sharpen.
